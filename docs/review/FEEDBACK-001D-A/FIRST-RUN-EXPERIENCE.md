# First-run experience

This is an expert/automation audit, not a study of first-time humans. Native runs used ordinary production Play with supported deterministic seed URLs and real keyboard input. The collector/pilot read current model positions/certificates to choose keys, giving them knowledge a novice would not have. Their survival and pickup counts are coverage evidence, not difficulty or satisfaction measures.

## Teaching priorities

1. Keep launch immediate and the center clear. Correct capability-aware controls first.
2. At the first readable fragment encounter, use a short peripheral prompt: Collect cyan fragments · Optional run count. Exact trigger and lifetime need playtesting; do not compete with damage or phase alerts.
3. Let existing ring, sound, +1 and counter teach the pickup. Confirm muted recognition before extending effects (UX-08).
4. A concise phase transition can explain faster flight without asking the player to interpret scheduling parameters.
5. Do not explain upgrades, economy, elaborate obstacle patterns or future collectible uses. They are intentionally postponed.

Known players should retain immediate replay and avoid re-reading a mandatory lesson. The existing hint repeats every run; a future first-run prompt can be session-aware without adding accounts or progression. Saving a tutorial flag is only justified if repeated prompts become intrusive.

## Human validation plan

Use five fresh players for an initial qualitative pass, with at least two touch users. Ask them to start unaided, explain what to avoid/collect, identify a successful pickup, describe protection after a hit, and restart. Observe before explaining. Record time to first intentional movement, wrong-way control attempts, pickup recognition, damage explanation and whether they find pause/results actions. Compare current build and the later small implementation on matched situations; do not infer statistical improvement from five participants. Repeat with known players to check that teaching does not interrupt flow. Include a motion-sensitive participant for comfort feedback under their preferred setting.
